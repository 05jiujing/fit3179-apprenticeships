Rebuilding the Nation: Apprenticeships and Traineeships Across Australia

An empirical investigation into Australia’s vocational education and training (VET) landscape: examining geographic disparities, historical subsidy cycles, chronic trade shortages, and completion bottlenecks.

Author: Ho Jia Jun Student ID: 34676406 Published: October 2026 Primary Sources: NCVER, ABS Labour Force (6202.0), JSA & Fair Work Commission
Section 1: Geographic Distribution

1. The Geographic Landscape of Australian Training

Australia’s apprentices and trainees are not distributed evenly across the federation. State economic structures, major infrastructure pipelines, and regional mining operations shape where vocational learners concentrate.

01 / Choropleth Map Specification ↗

Apprentice Density by State and Territory

Apprentices in-training per 1,000 employed workers across Australian jurisdictions (2021–2025).

Source: NCVER, Apprentices and trainees, December quarter 2025 & ABS Labour Force (6202.0).

Colour shows apprentices in training per 1,000 employed people. Tasmania ranks highest every year (47.1 in 2022, 30.4 in 2025), and Queensland leads the mainland. Drag the year to see every state fall after 2022.
02 / Proportional Symbol Map Specification ↗

Regional Training Hubs & Trade Focus

Active apprentices and trade concentration across major training centres.

Source: NCVER & ABS Regional ASGS geographic data.

Each circle is a training hub, sized by learners. Capitals are largest, but resource regions such as the Pilbara have the highest trade share. Use the dropdown to isolate remoteness.
03 / Equal-Area Spatial Cartogram Specification ↗

Contraction of Commencements by State (2021–2025)

Equal-area hex cartogram showing the percentage fall in new apprentice commencements from the subsidy peak to 2025.

Source: NCVER, Apprentices and trainees collection.

Each hexagon is one state or territory, the same size so small places are visible. Commencements fell in every jurisdiction from December 2021 to December 2025, from −11.0% in the Northern Territory to −57.1% in South Australia.
Section 2: Longitudinal Evolution

2. Decades of Policy Shocks & The Subsidy Rollercoaster

Over the past decade, federal incentive structures have caused severe enrolment volatility—from the baseline pre-COVID years to the emergency 50% wage subsidies deployed during the pandemic.

04 / Connected Scatterplot Specification ↗

Commencements vs completions, 2016–2025

Each dot is one quarter; the line joins them in time order.

Source: NCVER, Apprentices and trainees, December quarter 2025.

Each dot is one quarter. Commencements surged from about 22,000 in 2020-Q2 to 99,410 in 2022-Q1, but completions stayed between 14,800 and 32,600 throughout. The wage subsidy filled the pipeline, not the exits.
05 / Diverging Area Chart Specification ↗

Net change in apprentices, by quarter

Quarterly balance of new commencements against cancellations, withdrawals, and completions (2016–2025).

Source: NCVER, Apprentices and trainees historical collection.

Above the line, more people joined than left. The biggest gain was +43,945 in 2022-Q1. The biggest loss was −30,870 in 2022-Q4, and numbers have fallen almost every quarter since, to 282,430 in December 2025.
Section 3: Sector Breakdown & Shortages

3. The Trade Bottleneck, Demand & Wage Realities

Australia’s national infrastructure pipeline and housing targets face severe constraints: traditional trade occupations remain locked in acute national shortage, while entry-level apprentice award wages lag behind the adult minimum living wage.

06 / Treemap Specification ↗

Apprentice Distribution Across Australian Industry Divisions

Tile area encodes active apprentice population; color saturation encodes trade share (%).

Source: NCVER National Collection & ANZSIC Industry Divisions.

Tile area shows apprentice headcount while colour indicates trade share. Construction dominates with 116,420 apprentices and 93.4% in trades, whereas Health Care has 34,890 learners but only 5.4% in trades. Vocational training remains deeply split between traditional trades and non-trade services.
07 / Slopegraph Specification ↗

Top 10 Trade Occupations: The Post-Boom Shift (2021 vs 2025)

Comparing quarterly commencements during the subsidy peak vs post-subsidy reality.

Source: NCVER, Apprentices and trainees quarterly collections.

Between December 2021 and December 2025, commencements fell 36.7% for carpenters (2,970 to 1,880) but only 1.4% for electricians (2,715 to 2,675). Electricians now start the most apprentices of any trade.
08 / Box Plot Specification ↗

Apprentice Award Wages vs National Adult Minimum Wage

Weekly statutory modern award wage progression (Stage 1 to Stage 4) against the national adult minimum.

Source: Fair Work Commission Modern Award Pay Schedules (2024).

Award pay for first-year apprentices sits well below the adult minimum wage of $915.90 a week, rising by stage.
Section 4: Diversity & Retention

4. Diversity, Demographics & The Completion Crisis

Who undertakes apprenticeships today, and who succeeds? The demographic profile is undergoing a generational shift, but completion rates in critical trades remain stubbornly low.

09 / Cleveland Dumbbell Plot Specification ↗

The Vocational Gender Divide: Female vs Male Share Across Disciplines

Gender distribution across active vocational contracts in South Australia.

Source: SA Skills Commission (October 2024).

In South Australia’s apprenticeship contracts, gender splits are stark: 1.8% of plumbing and 5.5% of electrician learners are women, but 96.3% of child care aides. Source: SA Skills Commission, October 2024.
10 / Streamgraph Specification ↗

The Aging Apprentice: Demographic Transformation (2014–2024)

Evolution of intake shares across youth, young adult, and mature adult cohorts over a decade.

Source: NCVER, Historical Apprentice Demographics.

Colours trace each age group’s share of apprentice commencements over a decade. Teenagers under 19 shrank from 38.4% in 2014 to 29.2% in 2024, while adults aged 25 and over rose to 36.2%. Apprenticeships are increasingly taken up by mature workers retraining mid-career.
11 / Matrix Heatmap Specification ↗

Completion Rates Across Sectors and Qualification Levels

Cohort completion percentages by Australian Qualification Framework (AQF) level and vocational sector.

Source: NCVER Completion and Attrition Matrix.

Completion rises with qualification level in every sector. Commercial cookery is lowest (43.8% at Certificate III) and Electrotechnology highest among trades (63.4% at Certificate III).
